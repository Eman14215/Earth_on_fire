What do hotspots mean?

MODIS and VIIRS detect thermal anomalies from satellites. A detection could represent an active fire, prescribed burning or another intense heat source. It does not confirm a wildfire.

General safety guidance

If you see smoke or flames near you, move away from the area and follow local emergency services. This platform does not replace official evacuation or hazard warnings.

📊

Reading the numbers

Detection counts can include repeated satellite views of the same site. FRP is measured in megawatts; chart comparisons should consider different sensor resolutions and overpass patterns.

MORE DETAILS

From NASA observations to transparent, explorable data

EARTH ON FIRE displays your team's existing cleaned NASA FIRMS data from MODIS and VIIRS. GitHub Actions update the source dataset; the server checks for changes and atomically rebuilds its local read-optimized database.

01

Data source

NASA FIRMS active fire / thermal anomaly products collected by the team's pipeline.

02

Cleaning

The pre-cleaned CSV is used as provided. All satellite observations are retained in a traceable local database.

03

Country mapping

Coordinates are matched against public-domain Natural Earth country polygons and assigned to African subregions. Low-resolution boundaries can omit small islands or misassign border points.

04

Visualization

Observation density is aggregated into 0.25-degree map cells. These are not fire perimeter, satellite pixel footprint or validated hazard predictions.

Limitations

This dashboard cannot determine live weather, fire spread, verified incident severity, exact wildfire count, exposure, or emergency risk from thermal detections alone. Confidence was normalized in the source CSV and is not directly comparable across sensors. The 10-day window changes with each source update.
